    Subject-independent EEG emotion recognition with explainable AI

    Emotion classifiers trained on EEG often fail on people they have never seen. Our team benchmarks 13 machine- and deep-learning architectures for valence classification on the DEAP dataset under strict leave-one-subject-out validation, and pairs every model with explainability diagnostics to see which channels and time windows drive its predictions.

    My part: I built and evaluated a CNN-BiLSTM with multi-head self-attention (1.18M parameters) with a fresh model per fold, analysed it with gradient saliency, and replicated a published explainable CNN-RNN, explained with SHAP.

    31subjects, held out one at a time
    1,240EEG trials · 32 channels
    79.92%mean LOSO accuracy, best team model

  2. OngoingCourse research · DSAI 403 · team of 3

    Nature-inspired optimization for battery health and remaining-life prediction

    Lithium-ion batteries lose capacity with every charge cycle, and knowing when one will reach end of life matters for EVs and grid storage. We are building an end-to-end pipeline for State-of-Health (SoH) and Remaining-Useful-Life (RUL) estimation in which particle swarm, grey wolf and differential evolution tune the hyperparameters of LSTM, GRU and Transformer models.

    Evaluation: a non-optimized baseline against the nature-inspired versions on MAE, RMSE and computational cost. A research paper with fully reproducible code is in preparation.

    3optimizers: PSO, GWO, DE
    3model families: LSTM, GRU, Transformer
    2targets: SoH and RUL

Case study · research

EEG emotion recognition, explained

A team benchmark of interpretable deep models that recognize emotional valence from EEG, evaluated on people the models have never seen.

31subjects, leave-one-subject-out
1,240trials, 40 per subject
32EEG channels at 128 Hz
1.18Mparameters in my CNN-BiLSTM

The problem

EEG signals vary strongly from person to person, so a model that looks accurate on a random split can fail on a new subject. Deep models are also black boxes. The team evaluated models on the DEAP dataset while holding out one whole subject at a time, and paired each model with explainability diagnostics.

What I built

  • CNN-BiLSTM with multi-head self-attention (PyTorch): two Conv1d blocks over the raw 32-channel signal, a two-layer BiLSTM, four-head self-attention and temporal pooling. 1,179,457 parameters, trained from scratch in each of the 31 folds.
  • Gradient saliency and attention maps per channel and per trial on held-out subjects, to see which electrodes and time windows the model relied on.
  • A CNN-RNN replication of an explainable model from the literature (TensorFlow/Keras, 4.4M parameters) with band-pass filtering, baseline removal and per-subject normalization, explained with SHAP over channel × time-segment groups.

Team XAI toolkit

Across the study the team used Captum (Integrated Gradients, DeepLift, feature ablation, saliency), SHAP, functional-connectivity heatmaps and MNE scalp topomaps.

Takeaway

Cross-subject EEG is a hard generalization problem: accuracy on individual held-out subjects ranged from 15% to 95% for my model. That spread is exactly why strict subject-level validation and explainability matter before anyone trusts an EEG classifier.
